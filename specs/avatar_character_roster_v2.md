# Avatar Tactical Card Game — Character Roster (v2)

**Purpose:** A cross-series candidate roster for the Avatar tactical card game. Version 2 expands the pool to 30 characters, explicitly includes key *Legend of Korra* heroes and villains, and preserves room for additional characters in later waves. This is a character-pool proposal, not a final balance sheet: each character should eventually have distinct attributes, a passive trait, and a signature card package.

## Roster principles

- **30 characters total** across five gameplay classes: Water, Earth, Fire, Air, and Non-bending.
- The roster is intentionally not perfectly even: characters are assigned by primary gameplay identity, not forced into equal class counts.
- Includes both *Avatar: The Last Airbender* (ATLA) and *The Legend of Korra* (LOK), with key LOK protagonists and antagonists represented.
- Each entry has a series, role, and release-wave designation so future additions can be planned without displacing the core roster.
- Tiny emoji-style glyphs are placeholders for UI icons. They are not official character art and can later be replaced with custom portraits or original silhouettes.
- Class means the character's primary gameplay identity. Avatars are assigned to their most defining base class for roster accounting; Aang is listed under Air, while his four-element access and Avatar State should be represented by special mechanics.

## Villain and control-mechanic guardrails

- Villains use the same action economy, stats, and resource rules as heroes; their identity comes from kit design, not hidden rule advantages.
- Bloodbending, chi-blocking, lightning, combustionbending, lavabending, flight, and spirit enhancement must each have visible tells and meaningful counterplay.
- Bending suppression should never permanently remove a character's core kit in standard matches. If a story-specific mode requires that outcome, implement it as an explicit scenario rule.
- Preserve future slots for additional LOK characters and antagonists, including Tarrlok, Unalaq/Vaatu, Ghazan, Suyin Beifong, and Iroh II, rather than treating this 30-character pool as a hard ceiling.

## Cross-class roster considerations

### Avatar State

- Aang is the only character in this roster with the Avatar State.
- Keep Avatar State separate from normal Air-class abilities and from Focus.
- Its build-up, activation cost, duration, bonuses, and immediate-loss condition should be implemented by the shared battle engine, not hard-coded into individual card effects.
- The roster may later add other Avatars from different eras. They should be clearly marked as Avatars and balanced around the same core risk rules, with character-specific variations only where necessary.

### Team-building constraints to test

- Prevent teams from stacking only high-control characters without giving opponents counterplay.
- Make sure each class has at least one focused attack, one defensive option, and one positional or utility technique across its card pool.
- Avoid class hard-counters: each class should have viable answers to every other class.
- Test mixed teams as well as mono-class teams.
- Consider restricting teams to one Avatar-capable character in standard play.

## Suggested MVP implementation order

1. **Core six:** Aang, Katara, Toph, Zuko, Sokka, Azula — a familiar and varied initial test roster.
2. **Core LOK additions:** Korra, Mako, Bolin, Asami, Lin, and one LOK antagonist such as Amon or Kuvira.
3. **Expand the cross-series pool:** add the remaining listed characters, including villains such as Fire Lord Ozai, Amon, Zaheer, and Kuvira.
4. Tune each character through playtests before adding more cards or complex passives.
